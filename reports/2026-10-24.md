# Report 2026-10-24: 0007, DFlash2 probabilistic drafting (a trade-off)

Patch: `0007-097-dflash2-probabilistic-v2`, on rdna-boosts `v12-097cf33d9-r59` (alone, or after `0001`-`0006`).
Written with Claude (AI).  R9700, Linux.

## What 0007 does

llama.cpp's `--spec-draft-sampling probabilistic` samples the draft and has the target verify it by rejection sampling.  The
draft-simple and MTP drafters already supply the candidate probabilities this needs; the DFlash2 drafter did not, so with a
DFlash2 draft the option did nothing.  0007 fills that in:

- For a request with temperature above 0, the draft path through DFlash2's selector lattice is sampled at every position
  (as `CandidateSelector.select` in the z-lab reference), and the 16 candidates with their probabilities go to the verifier.
- The candidates are first cut as the default sampler chain would cut them (top-k, top-p on the untempered probabilities,
  min-p), then softmax(score / temperature).
- One random generator per sequence, reseeded from the request seed; the candidates are walked in token-id order (the device
  top-k does not fix their order), so a fixed seed reproduces the output.

The output follows the same distribution as without 0007, but it is not the same tokens: the verifier draws differently.
Greedy requests and the default greedy drafting are untouched, and nothing changes unless the server is started with
`--spec-draft-sampling probabilistic`.

## Distribution check

4 short prompts (English story, business, Python, Chinese fiction), seeds 1-500 each, temperature 1.0 / top-k 40, 10 tokens;
reference: drafting that accepts only an exact match (exact by definition).  Per-position chi-square of the token
distribution at positions 2-10: 1 of 36 tests below p = 0.05 (1.8 expected by chance), smallest p 0.0037 (Bonferroni
threshold 0.0014); the joint (2, 3) distribution: all 4 above p = 0.49.  Same with `--backend-sampling`.  No sign of a bias.

## Speed: a trade-off

Production setup: Qwen3.8-27B UD-Q4_K_XL, DFlash2 draft (Q4_K_M, 5 tokens), 2 slots, `-c 245760`, q8_0 KV; one fresh server per
arm, arms alternating, two rounds.  Time per step is the same with and without 0007; what changes is how many drafted tokens
the target accepts.  Tokens/s against greedy drafting:

| workload | r59 (0007 without the candidate cut) | r115 (0007 as here) |
|---|---|---|
| coding prompt, chat with reasoning, temperature 0.7 | **+30.6%** (3.24 vs 2.47 tokens per step) | +4.6% (2 seeds) |
| same prompt, temperature 1.0 | **+13.1%** | **+17.1%** |
| grade-school math, temperature 1.0 | **+11.5%** | — |
| long-text continuation, temperature 0.7 | −1.8% [−8.3, +4.2] (48 texts) | English +12.1%, documents +6.7%, Chinese −6.5% |
| tool-calling ERP requests, temperature 0.7 | −1.3% (noise) | +4.9% [−4.3, +14.7] |
| Chinese fiction, temperature 0.3 | +10% | −3.4% |
| recorded coding-agent turns, temperature 0.3 / top-p 0.9 | — | **−3.9% [−7.1, −0.6]** |
| greedy (temperature 0) | unchanged | unchanged |

Brackets: bootstrap 95% intervals of the per-request ratio.  The r115 column is the patch as shipped here; on r59 the
candidate cut was not in yet, and the cut alone measured −0.4% to +2.5% against the uncut version on r115 (every interval
includes 0).  The large r59 gains at temperature 0.7 did not repeat on r115: the base's outputs differ, and with two seeds one
trajectory moves the number a lot.

Where it loses: at low temperature the target is far more certain than the draft, so a sampled draft position often picks a
candidate the target would not draw; the rejection then drops the rest of the draft.  Greedy drafting does not have this
problem.

## When to turn it on

- Sampled requests at temperature 0.7-1.0 (code, math, reasoning chats): likely faster.
- Low-temperature agent work (0.3) and mostly-greedy use: leave it off.
- Mixed or unknown workloads: measure on your own requests first.

## Applicability

The patch applies to plain r59 on its own and after `0001`-`0006` (`git apply`); the tree after `0001`-`0007` builds.  The
same code is in our r115 and r123 builds.
